## 📝 Ingredients
- **3 lbs fresh green beans** – washed, trimmed, and snapped    
- **8–12 slices bacon** – thick-cut    
- **8–10 small potatoes** – diced (red or Yukon Gold)    
- **1 medium yellow onion** – chopped    
- **1 tbsp garlic** – minced    
- **2 cups chicken broth**    
- **2 tsp sugar** _(optional)_    
- **1 tbsp Creole seasoning (Creole Kick)**    
- **1 tsp salt** _(adjust to taste)_    
- **1 tsp black pepper** – freshly cracked    
---
## 🍳 Instructions

### **1. Prep the vegetables**
- Rinse and trim green beans; snap into bite-size pieces    
- Peel and dice potatoes into chunks    
- Chop onion and mince garlic    

---
### **2. Cook the bacon**
- Heat a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat    
- Cook bacon until crispy    
- Remove bacon and drain on a paper towel    
- Leave **2 tbsp bacon grease** in the pot    

---
### **3. Sauté aromatics**
- Add chopped onion to bacon grease    
- Cook until translucent (about 5 minutes)    
- Add minced garlic; cook 1–2 minutes (don’t burn)    
---
### **4. Add vegetables**
- Add green beans + potatoes to the pot    
- Stir to coat them in the onion-garlic mixture    

---
### **5. Season and add broth**

- Add:    
    - Creole seasoning
    - Salt        
    - Pepper        
    - Sugar (optional)        
- Pour in **2 cups chicken broth**    
- Add a little water if green beans are not mostly submerged    

---
### **6. Add bacon & simmer**

- Crumble **half** the bacon and stir it into the pot    
- Cover, reduce heat to **low**, and simmer **45–60 minutes**    
- Stir occasionally to prevent sticking    

---
### **7. Finish & serve**
- Taste and adjust seasoning    
- Transfer to serving bowl    
- Sprinkle remaining bacon on top    

---
## 🍽️ Tips
- Use **fresh** green beans for best texture    
- Thick-cut bacon adds deeper flavor    
- If the broth reduces too much, add a splash of water    
- For smoky flavor: add a drop of liquid smoke or smoked paprika    
- This reheats beautifully for holidays    

---
## 🔗 Notes
- Perfect side dish for Thanksgiving, Christmas, and Sunday dinners    
- Works well with smoked turkey instead of bacon
